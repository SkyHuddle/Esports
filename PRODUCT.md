# Product: Esports Draft Games

## Product purpose
Three viral esports draft games — Ring Chase (CoD), Golden Road (LoL), Major Run (CS2). Each follows the same core loop: spin a historical team-year card, draft one player per round, get a season/tournament result, share it. Designed to create the "I got robbed" and "my lineup is better than yours" moments that make sports games spread.

## Register
product (app UI — design serves the game experience)

## Users
- Primary: hardcore and casual esports fans, 18–35, mobile-first, browse during tournaments or downtime
- Secondary: casual sports-game players who know 82-0 or Wordle and want something esports-flavored
- Context: playing 1–3 minutes at a time, often on a phone, often sharing results to X, Discord, Reddit

## Brand tone
Competitive, sharp, premium. Like ESPN if ESPN were made by people who watch esports. Not cartoonish, not childish, not tryhard neon-gamer. Think ESPN BottomLine meets a great sports betting app — dense, confident, legible. Data is king. Results feel real.

## Anti-references
- Neon-on-black gamer aesthetic (Razer, Alienware)
- Candy-colored mobile casual games
- Corporate SaaS dashboards with hero metrics
- Glassmorphism-heavy "dark mode portfolio"
- Rounded everything, pastel, Dribbble-style

## Core screens
1. Hub — pick a game
2. HomeScreen per game — start free / start daily, previous result, stats
3. DraftScreen — round-by-round pick flow (spin → pick)
4. ResultScreen — season/run result with share card
5. ShareCard — the artifact users screenshot and post

## Strategic principles
- Speed above all: open → understand → play → result → share in under 2 minutes
- The result must feel debatable. Users need to say "that's wrong"
- Daily creates habit. Free mode creates exploration
- Share cards are the viral engine. They must be worth screenshotting
- Mobile is primary. Desktop is fine but secondary
